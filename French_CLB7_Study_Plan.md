# French A0 → CLB 7 Study Plan
**Start:** July 18, 2026 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Target:** January 31, 2027 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **28 weeks, 4 hrs/day**

---

## First things first: book your exam slot NOW

This is the part people underestimate. TEF Canada slots often have **1–3 month wait times**, and TCF Canada runs in quarterly sessions with registration opening about a month ahead — popular centres fill within days. Your prep pace is not the bottleneck; **finding a seat is.**

**Action this week:**
1. Decide TEF Canada vs. TCF Canada (both map to CLB the same way — pick whichever centre near you has better availability).
2. Check your nearest Alliance Française or accredited centre's session calendar.
3. **Book your primary attempt for early-to-mid November 2026.** This gives you ~16 weeks of prep, a real test under your belt, and 2+ months of runway if you need to retake before January 31.
4. If your centre allows it, tentatively pencil in a **January retake slot** the same day you book November — cancel it later if you don't need it. Retakes have no mandatory waiting period for TEF; TCF requires ~30 days between sittings.
5. Note results turnaround: TCF results take 2–4 weeks; factor that into how late a January retake can realistically be.

## Daily 4-Hour Structure (use this from Day 1)

| Block | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 min | **Structured grammar/course** — textbook or app-based, new material |
| 2 | 60 min | **Listening immersion** — podcasts, news, shows (level-appropriate) |
| 3 | 60 min | **Speaking practice** — tutor, language exchange, or shadowing/recording yourself |
| 4 | 60 min | **Reading + writing** — texts + short production exercises |

Speaking and writing are the two skills self-study most easily neglects — and Speaking has the *highest* score threshold for CLB 7 on the TEF. Do not let Block 3 become "optional when I'm tired." If anything, protect it first.

---

## Phase 1 — Foundations (Weeks 1–4): A0 → A1

**Goal:** Basic sounds, present tense, core 500–800 words, simple sentences.

- **Grammar/course:** Duolingo (daily) + a structured beginner course — *Français avec Pierre* (YouTube, free) or *Alter Ego A1* textbook
- **Listening:** *Français Authentique* beginner episodes, slow French podcasts (InnerFrench "beginner" tagged episodes)
- **Speaking:** Start a weekly italki or Tandem partner session from **Week 1**, even if it's just numbers/introductions. Shadow short audio clips daily.
- **Reading/Writing:** *Le Point du FLE* A1 exercises; write 5 sentences a day about your day.

**Milestone check (end of Week 4):** Can you introduce yourself, describe your daily routine, and understand slow, simple spoken French with visual context? If not, extend this phase by a week before moving on.

---

## Phase 2 — Building (Weeks 5–9): A1 → A2

**Goal:** Past tense (passé composé, imparfait), future, ~1500 words, short conversations.

- **Grammar:** *Alter Ego A2*, or Lawless French A2 grammar notes
- **Listening:** RFI Savoirs "Journal en français facile" (daily news, simplified)
- **Speaking:** 2x/week tutor sessions; describe past events and weekend plans
- **Reading/Writing:** Short articles from *1jour1actu*; write a paragraph daily (diary style)

**Milestone check:** Can you tell a simple story in the past tense and understand the gist of slow news broadcasts?

---

## Phase 3 — Expanding (Weeks 10–15): A2 → B1

**Goal:** Opinions, hypotheticals, subjunctive basics, connected speech comprehension.

- **Grammar:** *Alter Ego B1*, subjunctive + conditional focus
- **Listening:** TV5Monde "7 jours sur la planète," French YouTube (unscripted, e.g., interviews)
- **Speaking:** 2–3x/week tutor sessions; debate simple opinions ("pour ou contre...")
- **Reading/Writing:** Short opinion pieces; write structured paragraphs with connectors (*cependant, donc, par conséquent*)

**Milestone check:** Can you express and defend an opinion, understand native-speed French on familiar topics, and write a coherent 150-word paragraph?

---

## Phase 4 — B2 Push (Weeks 16–19): B1 → B2

**Goal:** Nuanced argumentation, abstract topics, exam-specific task formats.

- **Grammar:** Fill remaining gaps (complex subjunctive, relative pronouns, register/formality)
- **Listening:** Native podcasts/news at full speed — France Inter, France Culture
- **Speaking:** Practice actual TEF/TCF speaking task formats (describing, persuading, debating) with your tutor
- **Reading/Writing:** Practice actual TEF/TCF writing tasks (formal letter, argumentative essay) under timed conditions

**Start exam-specific prep materials here:** official CCI Paris IdF "Réussir le TEF" books, or TCF/TEF-specific platforms (e.g. PrepMyFrench) for realistic mock scoring.

**Milestone check:** Full timed mock exam — identify your weakest skill 3–4 weeks before your real test date.

---

## Phase 5 — Exam Sprint & Test Window

- Final 1–2 weeks before your booked date: mostly timed mock exams + targeted fixes on your weakest skill
- Sleep and rest the 2 days before the real exam — cramming grammar the night before helps less than being sharp for the Speaking section
- **Take your booked exam.**

---

## Phase 6 — Reinforce / Retake Buffer (post-exam → Jan 31)

- If all four skills clear CLB 7 — you're done early. Nice.
- If one or two skills fall short: this buffer exists exactly for that. Concentrate almost all study time on the specific weak skill(s) rather than general review.
- Retake with enough runway before Jan 31 to receive results (TCF: 2–4 weeks; TEF: check your centre).

## The one thing that will make or break this

Consistency beats intensity. Four hours today and zero tomorrow is worse than 3+3 every day. If a day gets disrupted, protect the speaking block above all — it's the hardest to make up later and the one skill threshold you're most likely to underperform on if neglected.
